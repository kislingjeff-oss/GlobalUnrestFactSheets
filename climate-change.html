Climate Change, Science and Policy

Jeff's posts on climate change, mostly from 2017–2022, follow the science (CO2 records, IPCC and U.S. assessments, temperature records) and the politics (the Paris Agreement, the Green New Deal and the Sunrise Movement, the Inflation Reduction Act, and continued fossil fuel expansion and financing). They also track extreme weather close to home, such as the 2019 Missouri and Mississippi River floods and the 2020 Iowa derecho, and disasters worldwide.

40 facts checked against outside sources, 3 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

Facts

1. 2008-10-10 (corrected)

In an October 2008 address, a UNHCR senior adviser on climate change said current estimates of people who would be forcibly displaced by climate change by 2050 ranged from 250 million to one billion.

Correction: Posts say UNHCR estimated up to 250 million; the UNHCR document gives 250 million as the low end of a range of estimates (250 million to one billion).

  • Source: Address by Mr. Yoichiro Tsuchida, UNHCR Senior Advisor on Climate Change, Bonn, 10 October 2008

2. 2015-11

In November 2015 New York Attorney General Eric Schneiderman subpoenaed Exxon Mobil, investigating whether the company misled the public and investors about the risks of climate change.

  • Source: NY attorney general investigates Exxon Mobil over climate claims (Fortune)

3. 2015-12-12

The Paris Agreement was adopted by 195 Parties at COP21 in Paris on December 12, 2015, and entered into force on November 4, 2016. It aims to hold warming well below 2°C and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.

  • Source: The Paris Agreement (UNFCCC)
  • In the posts: No War! No Warming! (2015-12-13); Emergence of a New Global Civilization (2016-12-27); Wet’suwet’en and business as usual? NOT (2020-02-03); Wet’suwet’en 11.27.2021 (2021-11-27)

Note: Posts say 'over $195 billion'; the 2019 report figure is $196 billion.

6. 2017-06-01

President Trump announced on June 1, 2017 that the United States would withdraw from the Paris Agreement. The U.S. formally notified the UN on November 4, 2019, and the withdrawal took effect on November 4, 2020.

Monsoon flooding in South Asia in the summer of 2017 killed more than 1,200 people across India, Bangladesh and Nepal and affected about 41 million people, at the same time as Hurricane Harvey's flooding in Texas.

8. 2018

In 2018 wind generated about 6.5 percent of U.S. electricity and solar about 2.3 percent, together roughly 8 to 9 percent, according to EIA data.

Note: Posts say about 8 percent in early 2019; the figures are for calendar 2018, and the solar share includes small-scale solar.

  • Source: Renewables Provided 18% of U.S. Power Generation in 2018 (POWER Magazine)
  • In the posts: Sunrise Movement succeeds in forcing discussion of climate policy (2019-03-23)

9. 2018-04

In April 2018 the monthly average carbon dioxide concentration at Mauna Loa Observatory reached 410.31 ppm, the first month in the record to exceed 410 ppm.

  • Source: April monthly average exceeds 410 parts per million for the first time in recorded history (Scripps Institution of Oceanography, The Keeling Curve)
  • In the posts: Coal Persists Despite Record CO2 levels (2018-12-06)

10. 2018-07-23

On July 23, 2018 the city of Kumagaya, northwest of Tokyo, reached 41.1°C (106°F), the highest temperature ever recorded in Japan.

  • Source: Japanese heat wave pushes temperature to record (The Week (AP))
  • In the posts: Climate Change on NBC (2018-08-04)

11. 2018-08-01

The State of the Climate in 2017 report (published August 1, 2018 in the Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society) found 2017 was the warmest non-El Niño year on record and the second or third warmest year overall; atmospheric CO2 averaged a record 405.0 ppm; and global sea level was rising about 1.2 inches per decade.

  • Source: International report confirms 2017 was among the three warmest years on record (NOAA NCEI)
  • In the posts: Climate Change on NBC (2018-08-04)

12. 2018-10-08

The IPCC Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5°C was approved in Incheon, South Korea and released on October 8, 2018. It found that limiting warming to 1.5°C requires global net CO2 emissions to fall about 45 percent from 2010 levels by 2030 and reach net zero around 2050.

Note: Posts paraphrase the finding as 'just 11 (or 12) years' to act, a popular reading of the 2030 target.

  • Source: Summary for Policymakers of IPCC Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5°C approved by governments (ScienceDaily (IPCC release))

18. 2019-02-07

The Green New Deal resolution was introduced on February 7, 2019: H.Res.109 by Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez in the House and S.Res.59 by Senator Ed Markey in the Senate, where it was referred to the Environment and Public Works Committee. It was a non-binding resolution, not a bill.

  • Source: H. Res. 109 (Introduced in House) (GovInfo); US SR59 | 2019-2020 | 116th Congress (LegiScan)

19. 2019-03

A powerful mid-March 2019 'bomb cyclone' caused record flooding on the Missouri River and its tributaries: 17 levees were breached or overtopped in Iowa, Nebraska and Missouri, Nebraska's Spencer Dam was destroyed, the Pine Ridge Reservation in South Dakota was cut off, and Nebraska alone estimated over $1.3 billion in preliminary damage.

  • Source: River flooding inundates the Northern Plains in spring 2019 (NOAA Climate.gov)

Swedish climate activist Greta Thunberg was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize in March 2019 by three Norwegian lawmakers, on the eve of the March 15, 2019 global school climate strike in more than 100 countries.

  • Source: Greta Thunberg nominated for Nobel Peace Prize (Electrek)

24. 2019-05-02

On May 2, 2019 the U.S. House passed the Climate Action Now Act (H.R.9) 231–190, with all Democrats and three Republicans voting yes. The bill barred funds for withdrawing from the Paris Agreement and required a plan to meet U.S. commitments under it.

  • Source: Roll Call 184, H.R. 9 (Clerk of the U.S. House of Representatives); House passes bill opposing Trump Paris Agreement withdrawal (Climate Home News)

25. 2019-09-20

The Global Climate Strike of September 20, 2019, timed three days before the UN Climate Action Summit, drew more than 4 million people worldwide; strikes during September 20–27 took place at about 4,500 locations in 150 countries. In Iowa, the strike gathered at the State Capitol in Des Moines.

  • Source: September 2019 climate strikes (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: You’re Invited: Global Climate Strike (2019-09-03); Iowa and Global Climate Strike (2019-09-17); Strike DC Press Release (2019-09-23)

26. 2020-08-10

On August 10, 2020 a derecho crossed eight Midwestern states over about 770 miles in 14 hours, with gusts estimated near 140 mph around Cedar Rapids, Iowa. It caused about $11 billion in damage, the costliest thunderstorm event in U.S. history, and damaged crops on roughly 14 million acres in Iowa.

  • Source: A year ago, the Midwest was hit with the costliest thunderstorm event in U.S. history (NPR via KUNC); 2020 Midwest derecho (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Exhilarated again (2020-08-11); Forget everything (2020-08-18); What kind of ancestor do you want to be? (2020-09-04); Racism as a Public Health Threat (2020-11-20)

27. 2020-12

The Copernicus Climate Change Service estimated that global warming reached 1.18°C above pre-industrial levels in December 2020, and that at the rate of the previous 30 years the 1.5°C level would be reached around 2034.

  • Source: How close are we to reaching a global warming of 1.5°C? (Copernicus Climate Change Service)
  • In the posts: Climate tipping points that might already have been reached (2021-04-29)

28. 2021-03

According to Rainforest Action Network's Banking on Climate Chaos 2021 report, JPMorgan Chase was the largest bank funder of fossil fuels since the Paris Agreement and provided $51.3 billion in fossil fuel financing in 2020.

29. 2021-08-09

The IPCC Working Group I report for the Sixth Assessment, released August 9, 2021, found that human greenhouse gas emissions have caused about 1.1°C of warming since 1850–1900 and that global temperature is expected to reach or exceed 1.5°C averaged over the next 20 years.

  • Source: Climate change widespread, rapid, and intensifying – IPCC

Note: Negotiations ran a day past the scheduled close; the pact was agreed on November 13, 2021.

35. 2022-08

The Inflation Reduction Act required the Interior Department to hold specified offshore oil and gas lease sales, including in the Gulf of Mexico, as a condition linked to new wind and solar leasing on federal lands, a provision that came from the compromise with Senator Joe Manchin.

  • Source: Inflation Reduction Act (Wikipedia); The climate effects of the Inflation Reduction Act (The Week)

36. 2022-08-16

President Biden signed the Inflation Reduction Act on August 16, 2022. Its climate and energy provisions included raising the 45Q carbon-capture tax credit (for example from $50 to $85 per ton for stored industrial CO2 and to $180 for direct air capture) and lowering eligibility thresholds.

  • Source: Inflation Reduction Act (Wikipedia); The Inflation Reduction Act Includes Significant Benefits for the Carbon Capture Industry (Gibson Dunn)
