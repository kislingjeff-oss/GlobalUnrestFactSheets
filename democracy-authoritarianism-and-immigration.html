Democracy, Authoritarianism and Immigration

These posts follow U.S. immigration enforcement and civil liberties from the 2016 election through the federal enforcement surge in Minneapolis in early 2026. They cover the 2017 travel ban, the 2018 family separation policy, ICE detention and Iowa's ties to it, DACA, and the January 6, 2021 Capitol attack. They also cover 2025 executive actions against campus protesters and student visa holders, the June 2025 National Guard deployment in Los Angeles, and Iowa's 2018 critical-infrastructure protest law.

39 facts checked against outside sources, 6 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

Facts

1. 2011-10–2016-12 (corrected)

About 18,000 Syrian refugees (18,007) were resettled in the United States between October 1, 2011 and December 31, 2016.

Correction: Posts give the period as 2011–2019; the source figure runs to the end of 2016, and resettlement continued at lower levels afterward.

2. 2016

Russia's Internet Research Agency ran a social media influence campaign around the 2016 U.S. election using fake American personas and bot networks; the Special Counsel's report (2019) noted Facebook's estimate that IRA content reached about 126 million people.

  • Source: What the Mueller Report Tells Us About Russian Influence Operations (Lawfare)

3. 2016-10-03

On October 3, 2016, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit upheld a lower-court order blocking Indiana Governor Mike Pence's attempt to stop the resettlement of Syrian refugees in the state, finding the policy discriminated by nationality.

  • Source: Federal Appeals Court Rejects Indiana's Efforts to Block Syrian Refugee Resettlement (ACLU)
  • In the posts: Indiana Under Pence (2016-10-05)

4. 2016-11-08 (corrected)

Early estimates after the November 2016 election put turnout at about 55 percent of voting-age citizens, described as a 20-year low; later U.S. Elections Project estimates put turnout at about 58 percent of eligible voters, roughly equal to 2012.

Correction: Posts give 55.4% of eligible voters as a 20-year low; that was an early estimate based on the voting-age population, and final estimates of turnout among eligible voters were higher.

5. 2016-11-08

In the November 8, 2016 presidential election Hillary Clinton won the national popular vote by nearly 2.9 million votes while Donald Trump won the Electoral College; she was the fifth candidate in U.S. history to win the popular vote and lose the presidency.

  • Source: Clinton wins US popular vote by widest margin of any losing presidential candidate (Christian Science Monitor)
  • In the posts: Challenged on Democracy (2016-11-12)

6. 2017-01-27

On January 27, 2017, President Trump issued the executive order "Protecting the Nation From Foreign Terrorist Entry Into the United States" (the first travel ban), suspending entry from seven Muslim-majority countries for 90 days and suspending all refugee admissions for 120 days, with Syrian refugees barred indefinitely.

  • Source: Trump's executive order by the numbers (Atlantic Council)
  • In the posts: Censure the President (2018-01-12); Reclaiming the Moral Narrative (2018-06-21); Language (2025-03-13)

7. 2018

ICE Enforcement and Removal Operations reported 158,581 administrative arrests in fiscal year 2018, including 138,117 people with criminal convictions or pending charges (up 10,125 from FY2017), and 256,085 removals.

  • Source: Fiscal Year 2018 ICE Enforcement and Removal Operations Report (U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement); ERO 2018 (U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement)

8. 2018-01-11

  • Source: White House doesn't deny that President Trump referred to Haiti, African nations as 's---hole' countries (CNBC)

9. 2018-04

In 2018 Iowa enacted Senate File 2235, which created the crime of "critical infrastructure sabotage" (covering facilities such as pipelines and utilities) as a class B felony punishable by up to 25 years in prison and a fine of $85,000 to $100,000; Governor Kim Reynolds signed it in April 2018 and it took effect July 1, 2018.

Note: The law dates from the 2018 legislative session, not 2017.

  • Source: Senate File 2235 (enrolled text) (Iowa Legislature); Gov. Kim Reynolds signs "sabotage" bill into law (Bold Iowa); Legislative wrap-up for 2018 (Farm Progress)

10. 2018-04-06

On April 6, 2018, Attorney General Jeff Sessions issued a memorandum directing U.S. Attorneys on the Southwest border to prosecute all illegal-entry referrals (the "zero tolerance" policy), which led to parents being prosecuted and separated from their children.

  • Source: DOJ announces zero tolerance policy on Southwest border (Immigration Policy Tracking Project); Trump administration family separation policy (Wikipedia)

11. 2018-06-20

On June 20, 2018, President Trump signed Executive Order 13841, "Affording Congress an Opportunity to Address Family Separation," directing that families be detained together and asking the Attorney General to seek changes to the Flores settlement to allow longer family detention.

  • Source: Executive Order 13841 directs families to be detained together and seeks modification of Flores settlement (Immigration Policy Tracking Project)

12. 2018-06-25

  • Source: Trump administration family separation policy (Wikipedia)

13. 2018-06-26

More than 5,500 children were separated from their parents under the Trump administration's 2017-2018 border policy; on June 26, 2018, U.S. District Judge Dana Sabraw ordered reunification within 30 days (14 days for children under five), noting the government had no system to track separated children.

  • Source: Trump administration family separation policy (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Intentional Incompetence (2018-06-22); Where are the babies and children? (2018-07-12); Reclaiming the Moral Narrative (2018-06-21)

14. 2018-07-10

Under the court-enforced Flores settlement (1997, as interpreted in a 2015 order), migrant children generally may not be held in detention for more than 20 days; on July 10, 2018, Judge Dolly Gee rejected the Trump administration's request to detain children with their parents beyond that limit.

  • Source: Judge Rejects Trump Administration's Bid To Detain Migrant Children Indefinitely (NPR (via WHYY))
  • In the posts: Where are the babies and children? (2018-07-12)

17. 2019-08

Iowa businesses received about $73 million from contracts with ICE and Customs and Border Protection between fiscal years 2008 and 2019, most of it going to Rockwell Collins of Cedar Rapids, according to an August 2019 Iowa City Press-Citizen analysis of USASpending.gov data.

18. 2019-11-12

The U.S. Supreme Court heard oral arguments on the Trump administration's rescission of DACA on November 12, 2019 (DHS v. Regents of the University of California); on June 18, 2020, it ruled 5-4 that the rescission was arbitrary and capricious.

  • Source: Department of Homeland Security v. Regents of the University of California (Wikipedia)

19. 2020-07-01 (corrected)

After arrests at a July 1, 2020 racial-justice protest, the Iowa State Patrol banned several protesters from the State Capitol grounds; in December 2020 U.S. District Judge Rebecca Goodgame Ebinger blocked enforcement of the bans as likely unconstitutional, and in August 2021 the state settled, withdrawing the bans and paying $5,000 to each of five plaintiffs.

Correction: A 2021 post says the ban was 'later declared illegal'; the court issued a preliminary injunction finding the ban likely violated protesters' rights, and the case ended in a settlement rather than a final ruling.

  • Source: Judge OKs Iowa Capitol protesters' settlement, dismisses ban (CBS2 Iowa / Associated Press)
  • In the posts: Racial justice protesters banned from Iowa Capitol in “stunning misuse of power” (2020-10-09); State of Intimidation (2021-08-26)

20. 2021-01-06

On January 6, 2021, supporters of President Trump stormed the U.S. Capitol to disrupt Congress's count of the 2020 Electoral College votes, delaying certification for several hours; the FBI estimated 2,000-2,500 people entered the building and more than 1,500 were later federally charged.

  • Source: January 6 United States Capitol attack (Wikipedia); Jan. 6 Committee Releases Final Report - All 845 Pages (Mediaite)
