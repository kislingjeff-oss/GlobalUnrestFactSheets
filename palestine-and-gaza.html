Palestine, Israel and Gaza

Posts on this topic, concentrated between October 2023 and mid-2025, follow the Gaza war and its background: the 1948 Nakba, the 1967 occupation and the blockade of Gaza since 2007, casualty figures reported by the Gaza Health Ministry and UN agencies, famine warnings, the ICJ and ICC proceedings, UN votes and ceasefires, and U.S. military aid. They also document Quaker responses (AFSC and FCNL), arms-embargo and solidarity campaigns, aid flotillas, and the killing of journalists, aid workers and individual Palestinians whose stories became widely known.

48 facts checked against outside sources, 5 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

Facts

1. 1948

In the 1948 Nakba ('catastrophe'), roughly 700,000 to 750,000 Palestinians fled or were expelled from the land that became Israel; Palestinians mark Nakba Day on May 15, the day after Israel's declaration of independence.

  • Source: Commemoration of the 75th anniversary of the Nakba at UN Headquarters in New York (United Nations (UNISPAL)); Nakba (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Nakba and the Middle East today (2024-06-16); Nakba and colonialism in the US (2024-06-20); Ongoing Nakba (2025-05-17); The Modern Face of Empire (2025-06-20)

2. 1948–1949

The American Friends Service Committee began working with people displaced by the 1948 war at the request of the United Nations, and in 1949 organized relief for Palestinian refugees in the Gaza Strip; AFSC describes itself as supporting humanitarian efforts in Gaza since 1948.

  • Source: Middle East Region (American Friends Service Committee); New York Times rejects Quaker ad for calling Israel's actions 'genocide' (American Friends Service Committee)
  • In the posts: New York Times blocks AFSC (2025-01-09)

3. 1949-12-08

UNRWA, the UN agency for Palestine refugees, was created by UN General Assembly Resolution 302 (IV) on December 8, 1949, and began operations on May 1, 1950.

  • Source: Who We Are (UNRWA)
  • In the posts: International Day of Peace (2024-09-22)

4. 1967-06

In the Six-Day War of June 5–10, 1967, Israel captured the West Bank including East Jerusalem (from Jordan), the Gaza Strip and Sinai (from Egypt), and the Golan Heights (from Syria); the West Bank, East Jerusalem and Gaza have been under Israeli occupation since.

  • Source: Six-Day War (Encyclopaedia Britannica); Six-Day War (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Time for America to recognize Palestine (2025-02-07); Last Chapter of Genocide (2025-03-24); Masafer Yatta and Center for Jewish Nonviolence (2025-04-24)

5. 2007-06

Israel imposed a land, sea and air blockade on the Gaza Strip in June 2007, after Hamas took control of Gaza, citing security concerns.

  • Source: Gaza Blockade (UN OCHA occupied Palestinian territory); Madleen: Israel warns it will stop aid yacht reaching Gaza (BBC News)
  • In the posts: Freedom Flotilla Madleen updates (2025-06-06); Madleen Stopped by Israel (2025-06-09)

6. 2010-05-31

On May 31, 2010, Israeli forces raided the Gaza-bound flotilla ship Mavi Marmara; nine activists were killed during the raid and a tenth died in 2014 after years in a coma.

  • Source: Gaza flotilla raid (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Humanitarian Aid Ship Attacked (2025-05-03); All part of the same genocide (2025-05-04)

7. 2014

In 2014 the ICC Prosecutor found a reasonable basis to believe war crimes were committed during the 2010 Gaza flotilla (Mavi Marmara) raid but declined to open an investigation because the potential cases were not of sufficient gravity.

  • Source: Judges ask prosecutor to reconsider Gaza flotilla decision – What you should know (Coalition for the International Criminal Court)
  • In the posts: All part of the same genocide (2025-05-04)

8. 2019–2028

Under a 10-year memorandum of understanding covering 2019–2028, the United States provides Israel $3.8 billion a year in military aid ($3.3 billion in Foreign Military Financing plus $500 million for missile defense).

  • Source: U.S. Security Cooperation with Israel (U.S. Department of State); Israel–United States military relations (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: A time of dark shame (2024-08-22)

9. 2022-05-04

On May 4, 2022 Israel's Supreme Court rejected a petition against evictions in Masafer Yatta, allowing the military to remove residents from parts of the area declared Firing Zone 918; roughly 1,000–1,200 Palestinian residents were at risk of expulsion.

Note: The posts' description of this as potentially the largest forced transfer in the West Bank since 1967 was not stated in the sources seen.

  • Source: Firing Zone 918 (Wikipedia); Israel's Supreme Court ruling permits eviction of 1,000 Palestinians in West Bank (SabrangIndia)

15. 2024-01-26 (corrected)

In its January 26, 2024 provisional measures order, the ICJ found that the rights of Palestinians in Gaza to protection from acts of genocide were plausible and ordered Israel to take measures to prevent genocidal acts; it did not rule on whether Israel is committing genocide. Former ICJ President Joan Donoghue later stressed that the Court had not found it plausible that Israel was committing genocide.

Correction: Posts (and an AFSC statement they quote) describe the ruling as finding Israel's actions 'plausibly genocidal' or that it is 'plausible' Israel is committing genocide; the Court's plausibility test applied to the rights asserted by South Africa.

  • Source: South Africa's genocide case against Israel (Wikipedia); South Africa v. Israel case page (International Court of Justice)
  • In the posts: The eyes of the children are looking at us now (2024-10-23); New York Times blocks AFSC (2025-01-09)
