Enslavement, Racism, Policing and Prisons

The posts tie the history of slavery in the United States (1619, the Thirteenth Amendment's punishment clause and Juneteenth) and racial terror lynching, as documented by the Equal Justice Initiative, to present-day police killings and mass incarceration. They follow Black Lives Matter-era events from Ferguson (2014) to George Floyd and the Chauvin verdict, the militarization of police, Confederate flag and monument removals, and racial-justice organizing in Indianapolis and Des Moines.

45 facts checked against outside sources, 4 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

Facts

1. 1619-08

In late August 1619 (believed to be August 25), about "20 and odd" enslaved Africans arrived at Point Comfort, Virginia, aboard the English privateer White Lion, the first documented arrival of Africans in English-occupied North America.

Note: Posts describe this as the first slave ship to reach what became the United States; the Park Service frames it as the first documented arrival in the English colonies.

  • Source: 400 Years of African American History (National Park Service)
  • In the posts: Juneteenth (2019-06-20); Forcing the government to do their part in removing statues to white supremacy (2020-07-05)

2. 1865-06-19

On June 19, 1865, Union Major General Gordon Granger issued General Order No. 3 in Galveston, Texas, announcing that all enslaved people in Texas were free, more than two years after the Emancipation Proclamation; the day is commemorated as Juneteenth.

Note: The 2019 post, quoting another source, places the arrival "one day in late June, 1865"; the 2020 post gives the exact date of June 19.

  • Source: The Original Juneteenth Document (U.S. National Archives)
  • In the posts: Juneteenth (2020-06-19); Juneteenth (2019-06-20); “So, let’s get free!” (2020-07-04)

3. 1865-12-06

The Thirteenth Amendment, passed by Congress on January 31, 1865, and ratified on December 6, 1865, abolished slavery and involuntary servitude "except as a punishment for crime whereof the party shall have been duly convicted."

  • Source: 13th Amendment to the U.S. Constitution: Abolition of Slavery (1865) (U.S. National Archives)
  • In the posts: Decolonial Journey 13 – Abolition (2025-08-07); Making New Worlds (2021-04-16)

4. 1877–1950

The Equal Justice Initiative has documented more than 4,400 racial terror lynchings of Black people in the United States between Reconstruction and World War II (1877–1950).

  • Source: Lynching in America: Confronting the Legacy of Racial Terror (Equal Justice Initiative)
  • In the posts: Nooses (2019-07-18)

5. 1877–1950

The Equal Justice Initiative's Lynching in America research counts roughly 4,000 racial terror lynchings of African Americans in twelve Southern states between 1877 and 1950 (4,075 in its current edition).

Note: The 2016 post rounds the figure to 4,000; EJI's current count for the twelve states is 4,075.

  • Source: Lynching in America: Confronting the Legacy of Racial Terror (Equal Justice Initiative)
  • In the posts: Racial Terrorism (2016-03-07)

6. 1963-05

During Birmingham's Children's Crusade in May 1963, more than 1,000 young marchers were jailed; nearly 1,000 had been jailed by the end of the first day, May 2, and about 4,000 students went to jail between May 2 and May 11.

Note: Counts differ by source and by day: HISTORY.com reports nearly 800 arrests on May 2, while the Zinn Education Project gives almost 1,000 jailed by the end of May 2 and about 4,000 over May 2-11.

  • Source: May 2-11, 1963: Children of Birmingham Fill the Jails (Zinn Education Project); Children's Crusade march in Birmingham (HISTORY.com)
  • In the posts: Civil Disobedience and the Civil Rights Movement (2017-11-15)

7. 1963-09-15

On September 15, 1963, the bombing of the 16th Street Baptist Church in Birmingham, Alabama, killed four girls: Addie Mae Collins, Carol Denise McNair, Cynthia Wesley and Carole Rosamond Robertson.

  • Source: 16th Street Baptist Church bombing (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Another time of cultural chaos (2020-04-21)

8. 1964-06-21 (corrected)

On June 21, 1964, civil rights workers James Chaney, Andrew Goodman and Michael Schwerner were murdered in Neshoba County, Mississippi, by members of the White Knights of the Ku Klux Klan.

Correction: The 2020 post dates the murders to 1963; they took place on June 21, 1964.

  • Source: Murders of Chaney, Goodman, and Schwerner (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Another time of cultural chaos (2020-04-21); Civil Disobedience and the Civil Rights Movement (2017-11-15)

9. 1965-03-07 (corrected)

On March 7, 1965 (Bloody Sunday), state troopers beat marchers who were crossing the Edmund Pettus Bridge in Selma, Alabama, on their way to Montgomery; John Lewis, then 25, led the march and suffered a fractured skull.

Correction: The post describes the march as 'going to Selma'; it set out from Selma toward Montgomery. Lewis was born February 21, 1940.

  • Source: Bloody Sunday (1965) (Wikipedia); John Lewis (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Another time of cultural chaos (2020-04-21)

10. 1968-02-08

On February 8, 1968, South Carolina highway patrolmen and a city police officer fired on student protesters at South Carolina State College in Orangeburg, killing Samuel Hammond Jr., Delano Middleton and Henry Smith (the Orangeburg massacre).

  • Source: Orangeburg massacre (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Another time of cultural chaos (2020-04-21)

By 1978 the American Friends Service Committee's board had adopted a position supporting the abolition of prisons.

  • Source: Reflecting on AFSC's history in the penal abolition movement (American Friends Service Committee)

12. 1997

The Pentagon's 1033 program, which transfers surplus military equipment to local law enforcement agencies, was created by Section 1033 of the National Defense Authorization Act of 1997.

  • Source: How Tanks Got to Main Street (Friends Committee on National Legislation); 1033 program (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Ferguson and Militarized Police (2018-08-09)

13. 2010 (corrected)

A 2010 Pew Economic Mobility Project report found that one in 87 working-aged white men was in prison or jail, compared with one in 36 Hispanic men and one in 12 African American men.

Correction: The post states the figures without a source or age group; they come from Pew's 2010 'Collateral Costs' report and refer to working-aged men.

  • Source: Collateral Costs: Incarceration's Effect on Economic Mobility (Irish Penal Reform Trust (summary of Pew report)); ABFE Criminal Justice
  • In the posts: Criminal Justice Reform Bill (2018-12-20)

14. 2013-04

The Moral Mondays movement began in North Carolina in April 2013, led by Rev. William Barber II, then president of the North Carolina NAACP, with civil-disobedience protests at the state legislature; the first, on April 29, 2013, ended in 17 arrests.

  • Source: Moral Mondays (Wikipedia)

Indy10 Black Lives Matter was founded in 2014 by a group of ten strangers who traveled from Indianapolis to Ferguson, Missouri, to protest the police killing of Michael Brown.

Note: The posts add that the group met on social media and took supplies to Ferguson; the source checked does not mention those details.

  • Source: 10 Things To Know About Indy10 Black Lives Matter (Indianapolis Monthly)

16. 2014-08-09

  • Source: Michael Brown killed by police in Ferguson, Missouri (HISTORY.com)

Tamir Rice, a 12-year-old Black boy holding a toy gun, was shot and killed by a Cleveland police officer outside the Cudell Recreation Center on November 22, 2014.

In 2015 the Equal Justice Initiative began a project to collect soil from every site where a person was lynched, starting in Alabama, so that the jars could form part of an exhibit on the history of lynching.

19. 2015-01-28

On January 28, 2015, a South Carolina judge vacated the 1961 trespassing convictions of the 'Friendship 9', who were sentenced to 30 days of hard labor after a sit-in at the whites-only lunch counter of McCrory's in Rock Hill.

  • Source: Judge Throws Out 'Friendship Nine's' Civil Rights-Era Conviction (NPR); Judge exonerates Friendship 9 activists 54 years after arrest (WUWF); Friendship Nine (Wikipedia)

20. 2015-05-18

  • Source: 1033 program (Wikipedia)
