War, Peace and Conscientious Objection

These posts trace U.S. conscription and Quaker resistance to it, from Civilian Public Service in World War II through the Vietnam-era draft, lottery and draft-card resistance, the 1948 and 1968 Richmond statements, the 1980 return of registration, and the automatic registration enacted in 2025. They also cover the Korean War and diplomacy with North Korea, the atomic bombings, the post-9/11 wars and the 2001 AUMF, U.S. military budgets, drone warfare and the Russian invasion of Ukraine, often as background for the New Conscientious Objector and Active Objection ideas.

43 facts checked against outside sources. Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

Facts

1. 1941–1947

Civilian Public Service (CPS) operated from 1941 to 1947 under the Selective Training and Service Act of 1940, assigning about 12,000 conscientious objectors to 'work of national importance under civilian direction' in camps run with the historic peace churches (Brethren, Friends and Mennonites).

  • Source: Alternative Service: Conscientious Objectors and Civilian Public Service in World War II (The National WWII Museum); Civilian Public Service (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Draft Resister Stories (2017-04-29); Another Mentor, Another Quaker Jailbird (2019-10-22)

2. 1942-06-04

Congress has formally declared war 11 times; the last declaration came during World War II (against Romania, June 4, 1942). Later wars, including Korea, Vietnam, Iraq and Afghanistan, were fought under congressional authorizations or resolutions rather than declarations of war.

  • Source: Declarations of War (U.S. Senate)
  • In the posts: Lost Ideals (2017-04-08); Peace in a culture of violence? (2020-10-21); War, Peace and the Environment (2019-05-28)

3. 1944–1946

Civil rights and peace activist Bayard Rustin was imprisoned from 1944 to 1946, at the federal prisons in Ashland, Kentucky, and Lewisburg, Pennsylvania, for violating the Selective Service Act as a conscientious objector.

  • Source: Bayard Rustin: The Inmate that the Prison Could Not Handle (National Archives (Rediscovering Black History blog))
  • In the posts: John Griffith–War Resistance (2017-12-06); The Duty to Resist (2022-04-19)

4. 1945-08-06

The United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima on August 6, 1945, and on Nagasaki on August 9, 1945, the only uses of nuclear weapons in war.

  • Source: Atomic Bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki (National Archives (The Unwritten Record blog))
  • In the posts: Remember Hiroshima and Nagasaki (2018-08-02); Remember Hiroshima (2018-08-06); Remembrance of the Victims of the Bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki (2018-08-09)

5. 1948-07

A national gathering of Friends at Earlham College in Richmond, Indiana, adopted the 'Advices on Conscription and War' on July 20–22, 1948, urging Friends not to unite with any Selective Service draft, approving civil disobedience under divine compulsion, and supporting those who opposed conscription by non-registration or by registering as conscientious objectors.

  • Source: Advices on Conscription and War (Richmond, Indiana, 1948) (Inward Light)
  • In the posts: Peacemaking: fill the spiritual void (2022-11-08); International Day of Peace (2023-09-21); Richmond Declaration on the Draft (2019-10-05)

6. 1950-06-25

The Korean War began on June 25, 1950. Fighting stopped with the Armistice Agreement of July 27, 1953, but no peace treaty has ever been signed, so the war has never formally ended.

  • Source: Armistice Agreement for the Restoration of the South Korean State (1953) (National Archives)
  • In the posts: Follow-up on North Korea (2022-06-22); Support Peace on the Korean Peninsula (2018-05-16); Korean War Memorial (2023-11-27)

7. 1954-06-01

Congress renamed Armistice Day as Veterans Day in 1954; President Eisenhower signed the change into law that year (the renaming took effect June 1, 1954).

  • Source: From Armistice to Veterans Day (National Archives (Prologue blog))
  • In the posts: Honoring all victims of war, including those who resisted (2022-11-19)

8. 1959-09-23

Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev visited Iowa on September 23–24, 1959, touring Roswell Garst's farm near Coon Rapids and Iowa State University, an episode later cited as a model of citizen and agricultural diplomacy.

  • Source: Soviet Leader Nikita Khrushchev Visits Iowa Farm (Teaching Iowa History (State Historical Society of Iowa))
  • In the posts: Swords into plowshares (2017-10-07); Bear Creek Friends Meeting and North Korea (2017-10-09); US-Korea Exchanges Possible (2017-10-10)

9. 1964-08-07

Congress passed the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution on August 7, 1964, after reported attacks on U.S. destroyers, authorizing President Lyndon Johnson to 'take all necessary measures to repel any armed attack'; later investigations found the second reported attack (August 4) likely never happened. The resolution was the legal basis for escalating the Vietnam War and was repealed in January 1971.

  • Source: Tonkin Gulf Resolution (1964) (National Archives)
  • In the posts: Stop the March to War (2019-05-09); Questions and observations (2022-03-11); Is Trump Yet Another U.S. President Provoking a War? (2019-05-18)

10. 1965-07

A July 1965 Selective Service orientation memo titled 'Channeling' described using deferments as 'pressurized guidance' to steer young men into occupations the government considered to be in the national interest.

  • Source: Channeling (Selective Service System, July 1965) (Edward Hasbrouck)
  • In the posts: Scattergood Journal – Beginning to plan next Moratorium Day (2017-11-10)

11. 1965-08

In August 1965 Congress passed, and President Johnson signed, a law making it a federal crime to destroy or mutilate a draft card, punishable by up to five years in prison and a $10,000 fine.

  • Source: Meet the First Vietnam War Protestor to Be Prosecuted for Burning His Draft Card (TIME); Old Gold: Steve Smith, following his conscience (University of Iowa (Iowa Now))
  • In the posts: Honoring all victims of war, including those who resisted (2022-11-19)

12. 1965-10-15

David J. Miller burned his draft card at an antiwar rally in New York City on October 15, 1965, becoming the first person prosecuted under the new draft-card law; five days later, on October 20, 1965, University of Iowa student Steve Smith burned his card at the Iowa Memorial Union, the first to do so on a college campus after the law took effect.

  • Source: Meet the First Vietnam War Protestor to Be Prosecuted for Burning His Draft Card (TIME); Old Gold: Steve Smith, following his conscience (University of Iowa (Iowa Now))
  • In the posts: Honoring all victims of war, including those who resisted (2022-11-19)

13. 1967-04-04

Martin Luther King Jr. delivered his speech 'Beyond Vietnam: A Time to Break Silence' at Riverside Church in New York City on April 4, 1967, exactly one year before his assassination.

  • Source: Beyond Vietnam (Martin Luther King, Jr. Research and Education Institute, Stanford University)
  • In the posts: When silence is not an option (2017-04-05); Martin Luther King, “Beyond Vietnam: A Time to Break Silence” (2021-04-08); 20 Years of Endless War: Special Silent Reflection (2021-09-06); Martin Luther King Jr was a radical (2022-04-05); Beyond Vietnam (2025-01-16)

14. 1967-04-28

Muhammad Ali refused induction into the U.S. Army in Houston on April 28, 1967, was convicted of violating Selective Service law that June, and had the conviction unanimously reversed by the U.S. Supreme Court in Clay v. United States on June 28, 1971.

  • Source: Clay v. United States (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Muhammad Ali and me (2016-06-04); Muhammad Ali and me (2020-09-11); Memorial Day 2017 – Vietnam War (2017-05-29)

15. 1968-10-11

The Friends National Conference on the Draft and Conscription, convened by the Friends Coordinating Committee on Peace at Earlham College in Richmond, Indiana, on October 11–13, 1968, issued the Richmond 'Declaration on the Draft and Conscription'. It reaffirmed the 1948 Advices, rejected draft reform ('the issue is not equal treatment under compulsion, but freedom from compulsion'), and urged resistance such as refusing to register and refusing war taxes.

  • Source: 1968 Richmond Declaration re Draft (Inward Light); Declaration on the Draft and Conscription (Illinois Yearly Meeting)
  • In the posts: Richmond Declaration on the Draft (2019-10-05); National Quaker Conference on the Draft 1968 (2026-02-07); International Day of Peace (2023-09-21)

16. 1969-10-15

The first nationwide Moratorium to End the War in Vietnam took place on October 15, 1969, with demonstrations and teach-ins across the United States; a second Moratorium followed on November 15, 1969.

  • Source: Moratorium to End the War in Vietnam (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Connections (2017-11-08); Intersection of Inner and Outer Light (2018-01-28); Fifty Years Ago (2019-10-17); Protest and Protect (2019-10-29)

17. 1969-12-01

The first Vietnam-era draft lottery was held on December 1, 1969, at Selective Service headquarters in Washington, D.C.; birth dates drawn at random set the order of induction, replacing the earlier 'oldest first' method.

  • Source: Vietnam Lotteries (Selective Service System)
  • In the posts: Looking Back (2019-08-18); The Supreme Court (2020-09-19); No Way Out but War (2022-05-24)

18. 1970-05-04

On May 4, 1970, Ohio National Guard soldiers fired on antiwar protesters at Kent State University, killing four students and wounding nine.

  • Source: 50 years ago, the Kent State shootings sparked student unrest across America (National Geographic)
  • In the posts: Another time of cultural chaos (2020-04-21); Kent State Massacre (2025-05-06)

19. 1973-06-30

The last U.S. draft call was issued on December 7, 1972, and the authority to induct men expired on June 30, 1973, after which the military relied on an all-volunteer force.

  • Source: Vietnam Lotteries (Selective Service System)
  • In the posts: Military Recruitment, Schools, Free Speech and Peace (2020-09-02); No Way Out but War (2022-05-24); Peace in a culture of violence? (2020-10-21)

20. 1980

The American Friends Service Committee (AFSC) has engaged with North Korea since 1980, when it sent the first delegation by a U.S. humanitarian organization to the North since the Korean War; from 1997 it ran an agricultural program with North Korean cooperative farms and scientists.

  • Source: A Quaker legacy of working for peace in Korea (American Friends Service Committee)
  • In the posts: AFSC’s work in North Korea (2018-03-21); Rising tensions with North Korea and farm diplomacy (2022-06-16)

21. 1980-07

Draft registration was suspended on April 1, 1975, and resumed in July 1980 for men born in 1960 and later; the registration requirement for young men has remained in effect since.

  • Source: Vietnam Lotteries (Selective Service System)
  • In the posts: Draft resistance and conscientious objection (2018-05-25); Another Mentor, Another Quaker Jailbird (2019-10-22); Vietnam War 50 years later (2025-04-30)

22. 2001

In 2001 Ro Song Cho, then deputy director of North Korea's agriculture ministry, visited Bear Creek Friends Meeting in Iowa during a seven-week U.S. tour; AFSC had begun bringing North Korean agricultural scientists to the U.S. in 1999.

Note: The NK News account does not explicitly name AFSC as host of the 2001 visit.

  • Source: Sowing the seeds of improving agriculture: Quakers in North Korea (NK News); A Quaker legacy of working for peace in Korea (American Friends Service Committee)
  • In the posts: Bear Creek Friends Meeting and North Korea (2017-10-09); Swords into plowshares (2017-10-07)

23. 2001-09-18

The Authorization for Use of Military Force (Public Law 107-40), allowing the President to use force against those responsible for the September 11 attacks and those who harbored them, was approved on September 18, 2001, and has since been used as the legal basis for a wide range of military operations.

  • Source: Public Law 107-40: Authorization for Use of Military Force (Congress.gov)
  • In the posts: Authorization for War (2017-07-06); House Repeal of AUMF and Friends Committee on National Legislation (FCNL) Advocacy (2019-05-22)

24. 2003-03-20

The U.S.-led invasion of Iraq began on March 20, 2003, justified largely by claims of Iraqi weapons of mass destruction; no WMD stockpiles were found, and U.S. forces completed their withdrawal on December 18, 2011.

  • Source: Iraq War (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Is Trump Yet Another U.S. President Provoking a War? (2019-05-18); Rumours of War (2016-06-24); Temporary Memorial for the Children of Gaza (2025-03-10)

25. 2004

AFSC's 'Eyes Wide Open' exhibit, first shown in Chicago in 2004, displayed a pair of military boots for each U.S. service member killed in the Iraq war, with shoes representing Iraqi civilian dead; it later traveled to 48 states and the District of Columbia.

  • Source: American Friends Service Committee (Wikipedia)

At their April 27, 2018 summit at Panmunjom, the leaders of North and South Korea signed a declaration stating that 'a new era of peace has begun' and committing to pursue talks aimed at formally declaring an end to the Korean War.

The Taliban took Kabul on August 15, 2021, as the Afghan government collapsed, and the last U.S. troops left Afghanistan on August 30, 2021, ending nearly 20 years of U.S. war there.

40. 2022-02-24

Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine on February 24, 2022; that day Ukraine ordered a general mobilization and barred men aged 18 to 60 from leaving the country.

41. 2022-12

The fiscal year 2023 National Defense Authorization Act, passed by the House on December 8, 2022, authorized $858 billion for national defense, $45 billion above the administration's request.

  • Source: House Passes FY 2023 NDAA That Authorizes $858B for National Defense (USNI News)
  • In the posts: FCNL: Reduce the Pentagon Budget (2023-02-19)

42. 2025-12

The fiscal year 2026 National Defense Authorization Act, signed into law in December 2025, requires the Selective Service System to register draft-eligible young men automatically using federal data instead of relying on self-registration; automatic registration is scheduled to begin after December 18, 2026, and does not include women.

Note: Sources checked confirm the law, the December 2025 enactment and the December 2026 start, but did not name the section number (the post says Section 535) and describe the covered ages as 18 to 26 (the post says 18–25).

  • Source: Automatic Draft Registration: Not Just a Bad Idea, It's the Law (Quaker House); Automatic military draft registration takes effect in December. Here's how it would work (ABC11)
  • In the posts: The draft and youth today (2026-09-01)
