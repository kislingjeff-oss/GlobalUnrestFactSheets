Wet'suwet'en, Unist'ot'en and Coastal GasLink

From January 2020 on, many posts follow the Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs' opposition to the Coastal GasLink pipeline. They cover the Unist'ot'en camp and healing centre, the Gidimt'en checkpoint, the RCMP raids of 2019, 2020 and 2021, and the 2020 solidarity rail and port blockades across Canada. They also discuss Delgamuukw, the 2020 Memorandum of Understanding, UN CERD statements, and the later prosecutions and court findings about land defenders and journalists.

44 facts checked against outside sources, 3 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

Facts

1. 1984-10-24

On October 24, 1984, 35 Gitxsan and 13 Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs filed the Delgamuukw (Delgamuukw-Gisday'wa) claim in the B.C. Supreme Court, seeking recognition of title and jurisdiction over about 58,000 square kilometres of northwestern British Columbia.

Note: One post gives the area as 55,000 km2; most posts give 58,000 km2.

  • Source: Delgamuukw v British Columbia (Wikipedia); Delgamuukw Case (The Canadian Encyclopedia)
  • In the posts: Wet’suwet’en and the law (2020-02-13); Defund Coastal GasLink (2021-12-22); OPEN LETTER TO PRIME MINISTER TRUDEAU AND PREMIER HORGAN RE: WET’SUWET’EN HEREDITARY CHIEFS’ OPPOSITION TO COASTAL GASLINK PIPELINE PROJECT (2020-01-26); Gitxsan Hereditary Chiefs Spookw, Gwininitxw, and Dawamuux Arrested (2020-02-29)

2. 1997-12-11 (corrected)

The Supreme Court of Canada decided Delgamuukw v. British Columbia on December 11, 1997. It held that Aboriginal title is an existing right protected by section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982, rejected the finding that it had been extinguished, and set out a test for proving title, but it ordered a new trial rather than ruling on title to the specific lands; that second trial never took place.

Correction: Several posts say the Supreme Court 'confirmed' or 'proved' Wet'suwet'en and Gitxsan title to their territories; the Court laid down the legal framework and ordered a new trial, without itself deciding title to the claimed lands.

  • Source: Delgamuukw v British Columbia (Wikipedia); Delgamuukw Case (The Canadian Encyclopedia)
  • In the posts: This battle is far from over (2021-10-30); Defund Coastal GasLink (2021-12-22); Wet’Suwet’en Hereditary Chiefs: No Access Without Consent (2020-01-15); Sample Letter to John Horgan (2020-01-17); Arrests on unceded territory, including journalist (2020-03-04)

3. 2010

Freda Huson of the Unist'ot'en house group built a cabin in the path of proposed pipelines in 2010 and began hosting culture camps there, the start of the Unist'ot'en camp; The Tyee dates the Unist'ot'en checkpoint itself to April 2009.

  • Source: At the Unist'ot'en Outpost, a Tight-Knit Group Readies for Police (The Tyee); LNG Pipeline Unist'ot'en Blockade (analysis) (The Tyee); 2020 Canadian pipeline and railway protests (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Wet’suwet’en update 10.6.2021 (2021-10-07); Truthsgiving (2021-11-24); Unist’ot’en Healing Centre (2020-01-23)

4. 2015

The three-storey Unist'ot'en Healing Centre, a land-based healing facility, was built in 2015 at kilometre 66 of the Morice West Forest Service Road near the Morice River (Wedzin Kwa).

  • Source: At the Unist'ot'en Outpost, a Tight-Knit Group Readies for Police (The Tyee); In photos: Wet'suwet'en matriarchs arrested as RCMP enforce Coastal GasLink pipeline injunction (The Narwhal)
  • In the posts: Unist’ot’en Healing Centre (2020-01-23); Rise Up With Us (2020-02-08); Truthsgiving (2021-11-24)

5. 2017

The RCMP's Community-Industry Response Group (C-IRG), formed in 2017 to police resource-extraction conflicts, carried out the Coastal GasLink enforcement actions and became the subject of an investigation by the Civilian Review and Complaints Commission for the RCMP.

  • Source: RCMP Actions on Wet'suwet'en Territory (The Tyee)
  • In the posts: Indigenous Land Defenders (2025-02-21)

6. 2018-10-01

LNG Canada, a Shell-led joint venture, made its final investment decision on October 1, 2018, for a roughly $40 billion liquefied natural gas export project at Kitimat, B.C., which the Coastal GasLink pipeline was built to supply.

  • Source: LNG Canada (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Wet’suwet’en Updates 2.3.2020 (2020-02-04); Escalating tensions between Wet’suwet’en and RCMP (2020-01-29); Wet’suwet’en update 1.30.2020 (2020-01-30)

7. 2018-12

Members of the Gidimt'en clan set up a second checkpoint (Gidimt'en Checkpoint) on the Morice River Forest Service Road in December 2018, and on December 14, 2018, the B.C. Supreme Court granted Coastal GasLink a temporary (interim) injunction against the blockades.

  • Source: LNG Pipeline Unist'ot'en Blockade (analysis) (The Tyee); Coastal GasLink pipeline (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Ways to Support Wet’suwet’en Peoples (2020-02-18); Wet’suwet’en Land Re-occupations and Likhts’amisyu (2020-03-10); Voices from Gidimt’en Checkpoint (2020-01-25)

8. 2019-01-07

On January 7, 2019, RCMP officers enforcing the injunction broke through the gate at the Gidimt'en checkpoint and arrested 14 people.

  • Source: LNG Pipeline Unist'ot'en Blockade (analysis) (The Tyee); 2020 Canadian pipeline and railway protests (Wikipedia); BC NDP Faces Internal Pressure to Change Course on Wet'suwet'en Crisis (The Tyee)

9. 2019-11-26

On November 26, 2019 the B.C. legislature unanimously passed Bill 41, the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act.

  • Source: Joint statement on B.C. Indigenous human rights legislation passing unanimously (Union of BC Indian Chiefs)
  • In the posts: Wet’suwet’en update 1.30.2020 (2020-01-30); STAND DOWN, COME IN (2020-02-10)

10. 2019-12-13

On December 13, 2019, the UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination (CERD) called on Canada to halt construction of the Coastal GasLink pipeline on Wet'suwet'en lands until free, prior and informed consent was obtained, to withdraw the RCMP and associated security forces, and to prohibit the use of lethal weapons against Indigenous peoples.

  • Source: Canada and BC violate UN 'injunction' while lawful protesters arrested and threatened with armed force (Lawyers' Rights Watch Canada)

11. 2019-12-20

On December 20, 2019, The Guardian published documents ('Canada police prepared to shoot Indigenous activists, documents show') indicating that RCMP commanders planning the January 2019 Gidimt'en raid had discussed 'lethal overwatch'.

  • Source: 2020 Canadian pipeline and railway protests (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Life and Death Struggles Continue Today (2019-12-29); Criminalization of Indigenous land defenders (2021-11-21); Wet’suwet’en updates 11/20/2021 (2021-11-20)

12. 2019-12-31

On December 31, 2019, B.C. Supreme Court Justice Marguerite Church extended Coastal GasLink's injunction, converting the interim order into an interlocutory injunction that authorized RCMP enforcement.

  • Source: 2020 Canadian pipeline and railway protests (Wikipedia)

13. 2020-01-04

On January 4, 2020, the Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs served Coastal GasLink with an eviction notice ordering its workers off the territory.

  • Source: B.C. orders Coastal GasLink to stop pipeline construction near protected wetlands (The Narwhal); 2020 Canadian pipeline and railway protests (Wikipedia)

14. 2020-02

Solidarity protesters blockaded the Port of Vancouver and Deltaport in February 2020; police enforcing a court injunction arrested 47 protesters who refused to stop obstructing the port.

Note: A February 10, 2020 post reported 'over 30' arrests at the port the previous night; the 47 figure is the total given by the source.

15. 2020-02-06

On February 6, 2020, Mohawks of the Bay of Quinte began a rail blockade near Tyendinaga Mohawk Territory, near Belleville, Ontario, halting CN's main Toronto–Montreal line in solidarity with the Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs.

  • Source: 2020 Canadian pipeline and railway protests (Wikipedia)

16. 2020-02-06

RCMP enforcement of the injunction from February 6 to 10, 2020 led to 28 arrests along the Morice West Forest Service Road: 6 at kilometre 39 (Feb. 6), 4 at the Gidimt'en camp (Feb. 7), 11 at kilometre 27 (Feb. 8) and 7 at the Unist'ot'en Healing Centre (Feb. 10).

17. 2020-02-10

On February 10, 2020, RCMP arrested Unist'ot'en spokesperson Freda Huson (Howilhkat) and clinical director Karla Tait, among seven people, at the Unist'ot'en Healing Centre while the women were drumming and singing in ceremony.

  • Source: Emotions High as RCMP Arrest Unist'ot'en Matriarchs (The Tyee); In photos: Wet'suwet'en matriarchs arrested as RCMP enforce Coastal GasLink pipeline injunction (The Narwhal)

18. 2020-02-13

On February 13, 2020, CN shut down its rail network in eastern Canada and VIA Rail suspended almost its entire passenger network because of the solidarity blockades.

  • Source: 2020 Canadian pipeline and railway protests (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Decentralized rail, bridge, port and highway blockades (2020-02-14); Wet’suwet’en 2.13.2020 (2020-02-13)

19. 2020-02-18

The House of Commons held an emergency debate on the rail blockades on February 18, 2020; the next day NDP MP Taylor Bachrach (Skeena–Bulkley Valley) pressed the government in Question Period to have the Prime Minister meet the Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs.

Note: Posts say Bachrach spoke in the emergency debate; the sources seen confirm the debate and his February 19 questions but not his participation in the debate itself.

On February 24, 2020, Ontario Provincial Police removed the Tyendinaga rail blockade and arrested 10 demonstrators.

22. 2020-02-24

On February 24, 2020, RCMP removed a solidarity rail blockade at New Hazelton on Gitxsan territory, arresting 14 people including Gitxsan hereditary chiefs Spookw (Norman Stevens), Gwininitxw (Yvonne Lattie) and Dawamuxw (Larry Patsy); the Gitxsan and Wet'suwet'en had been co-plaintiffs in Delgamuukw.

23. 2020-03-01

On March 1, 2020, Chief Woos, federal Crown-Indigenous Relations Minister Carolyn Bennett and B.C. Indigenous Relations Minister Scott Fraser announced in Smithers a tentative arrangement on Wet'suwet'en rights and title, to go to the 13 house groups for approval; none of the parties changed their position on Coastal GasLink.

  • Source: 'It's not over': Tentative agreement with Wet'suwet'en Nation may be a milestone but it doesn't resolve pipeline fight (Ricochet); 2020 Canadian pipeline and railway protests (Wikipedia)

24. 2020-03-04

On March 4, 2020, police arrested five members of Indigenous Youth for Wet'suwet'en who were occupying an office in the B.C. Legislature in Victoria.

  • Source: B.C. legislature sit-in arrests (CBC News)

25. 2020-05-14

On May 14, 2020, Canada, British Columbia and the Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs signed a Memorandum of Understanding recognizing that Wet'suwet'en rights and title are held by the houses under their own system of governance; the MOU states that it does not address the Coastal GasLink pipeline.

  • Source: 2020 Canadian pipeline and railway protests (Wikipedia); Wetʼsuwetʼen (Wikipedia)
  • In the posts: Virtual Rally with Freda Huson and Molly Wickham (2020-05-05); Wet’suwet’en Response to Drilling (2022-10-16); Dear allies of the Wet’suwet’en struggle for sovereignty (2022-10-21)

26. 2020-06

In June 2020 the B.C. Environmental Assessment Office issued non-compliance orders requiring Coastal GasLink to stop construction near designated wetlands until it completed the surveys and planning required by its wetlands management plan.

  • Source: B.C. orders Coastal GasLink to stop pipeline construction near protected wetlands (The Narwhal)
  • In the posts: Noncompliance order for Coastal GasLink Pipeline (2020-07-08)

27. 2020-06-05

Charges against 22 Wet'suwet'en land defenders and supporters arrested in the early-2020 enforcement were dropped on June 5, 2020.

  • Source: B.C. orders Coastal GasLink to stop pipeline construction near protected wetlands (The Narwhal)
  • In the posts: Noncompliance order for Coastal GasLink Pipeline (2020-07-08)

28. 2021-09

From late September 2021, Gidimt'en members and supporters occupied Coastal GasLink's planned drill pad for the crossing under Wedzin Kwa (Morice River), setting up what became known as Coyote Camp, just upriver from the Unist'ot'en Healing Centre.

  • Source: Wet'suwet'en and Coastal GasLink explainer (The Narwhal); BC NDP Faces Internal Pressure to Change Course on Wet'suwet'en Crisis (The Tyee)

29. 2021-09-22

On September 22, 2021, Coastal GasLink cleared an archaeological site near the confluence of Lamprey Creek and Wedzin Kwa (Morice River) on Gidimt'en (Cas Yikh) territory under a site alteration permit from the BC Oil and Gas Commission.

  • Source: Wet'suwet'en and Coastal GasLink explainer (The Narwhal)
  • In the posts: Wet’suwet’en struggle continues (2021-09-27); Defending Wedzin Kwa (2021-09-28)

30. 2021-09-30

Canada's National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, created by Bill C-5 (royal assent June 3, 2021), is observed each September 30, the same date as Orange Shirt Day; its first observance in 2021 coincided with arrests at the Coastal GasLink drill-site road on Wet'suwet'en territory that the posts describe.

  • Source: National Day for Truth and Reconciliation (Government of Canada)
  • In the posts: National Day for Truth and Reconciliation (2021-09-30); Orange Shirts and Wet’suwet’en Arrests (2021-10-02); We fight for our children (2021-10-04)

31. 2021-10-27

On October 27, 2021, RCMP arrested Wet'suwet'en Likhts'amisyu hereditary chief Dsta'hyl (Adam Gagnon) and Gitxsan land defender Kolin Sutherland-Wilson on the Shea Forest Service Road southwest of Houston, B.C., after a blockade of a Coastal GasLink work camp.

  • Source: Arrest of Wet'suwet'en Hereditary Chief 'So Insulting' (The Tyee)

32. 2021-11-18

In RCMP raids on November 18 and 19, 2021, at least 30 people were arrested at the Gidimt'en camp and the Wedzin Kwa drill site, including Sleydo' (Molly Wickham); by early 2022 about 75 people had been arrested on Wet'suwet'en territory since 2019.

  • Source: BC NDP Faces Internal Pressure to Change Course on Wet'suwet'en Crisis (The Tyee); Court Convicts Indigenous Land Defenders, Will Hear Alleged RCMP Violations (The Tyee)

33. 2021-11-19 (corrected)

Two journalists, photojournalist Amber Bracken and documentary filmmaker Michael Toledano, were arrested and detained by the RCMP during the November 19, 2021 raid; Bracken later sued the RCMP over her arrest.

Correction: One post (relaying a camp update) said three accredited journalists were arrested; the sources report two.

34. 2022-07

In June and July 2022 the BC Prosecution Service approved criminal contempt of court charges against 19 people arrested in November 2021; after the January 2019 arrests the Crown had declined to proceed with charges.

  • Source: More Criminal Charges in Gas Pipeline Conflict (The Tyee); Court Convicts Indigenous Land Defenders, Will Hear Alleged RCMP Violations (The Tyee)

35. 2023-10

Coastal GasLink pipeline installation was completed in October 2023, and the pipeline entered commercial service in November 2024.

On January 12, 2024, Sleydo' (Molly Wickham), Shaylynn Sampson and Corey Jocko were found guilty of criminal contempt for defying the Coastal GasLink injunction during the November 19, 2021 arrests; the court then heard their application to stay the charges over alleged RCMP misconduct.

  • Source: Court Convicts Indigenous Land Defenders, Will Hear Alleged RCMP Violations (The Tyee)
